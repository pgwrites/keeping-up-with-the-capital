Hello there, stranger! 👀

Here for a sip?

Your favourite tea from Sand Hill Road to Koramangala is poured, and this week, everyone's whispering about what's actually inside Anthropic's books.

For three editions, I've written about Anthropic's IPO from leaks and reports. This week, the prospectus itself went public. It answered the question I kept coming back to: does this business make money? For one quarter, yes. But it has also signed up for a mountain of computing bills. In India, a batch of listed startups opened their own books, and most of them were in the black. <mark>So when a company finally shows you its numbers, what do you read first: the profit, or the bills?</mark>

Let's get to it.

## The Big Sip

# Anthropic's prospectus shows its first quarterly operating profit, and $417 billion in computing bills it can't cancel
{: .take}

On Monday, [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/) [made its IPO prospectus public](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/anthropic-publicly-files-ipo-prospectus-noting-strong-revenue-growth-and-massive-spending-131807138.html). It will trade on Nasdaq as ANT, and it's aiming to list in the week of 16 November, [per Dealroom's read of the filing](https://dealroom.co/news/info-1sghj5n-anthropics-s-1-takeaways-from-giant-compute-commitments-to-complex-lock/).

The headline is growth. Anthropic made $16.3 billion in revenue in the first half of 2026, [the filing shows](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm). In the same six months last year, it made $1.2 billion. That's 14 times more. And in the second quarter, it made an operating profit of $974 million, [per Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/info-1sghj5n-anthropics-s-1-takeaways-from-giant-compute-commitments-to-complex-lock/). That's the first operating profit in the six quarters the filing shows, [Linas Beliūnas noted](https://linas.substack.com/p/anthropic-ipo-s1-analysis-valuation) in his fintech and AI newsletter.

The profit came as computing costs fell relative to sales. In early 2025, Anthropic spent two and a half times its revenue on computing power. By the second quarter of this year, it spent less than half, Dealroom's figures show.

Then there are the bills. Anthropic has signed up for about $657 billion of computing over the next seven to ten years, Dealroom counted. It can't cancel $417 billion of that. That's about nine times what it now brings in a year, at its second-quarter pace.

Not everyone reads these pages the same way. Daniel McCarthy, a marketing professor at the University of Maryland's Smith School of Business, called the filing a strong positive. <mark>"The business is profitable when it's utilized. Cost per token is going down quickly,"</mark> he wrote, [per Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/anthropic-s-s-1-draws-reactions-investor-says-100-b-arr-could-be-possible-by-year-end/cZUIsqyRBzg). But Torsten Slok, Apollo's chief economist, sees a problem on the customer side. Outside big tech, he wrote, companies see <mark>"token spending not as a source of growth but as a cost center that weighs on margins,"</mark> [The Wealth Advisor reported](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/apollo-economist-says-ai-has-delivered-zero-financial-payoff-outside-big-tech). If he's right, the revenue that has to cover those bills could grow more slowly than the bills do.

[OpenAI](https://openai.com/) is watching the clock. It cut its target to a $1.25 trillion valuation for its round of up to $30 billion, and asked investors to commit by the end of this week, [Winbuzzer reported](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/10/27/openai-lowers-valuation-target-to-1-25-trillion-xcxwbn/), citing Bloomberg and the Financial Times. The FT also reported weaker demand and some fatigue among earlier investors. OpenAI wants to close before Anthropic lists, because both are pitching the same pool of money.

**Anthropic has shown it can make money. Its price now depends on whether revenue keeps outrunning bills it has already signed.**

## Connecting the Dots

<div class="pattern" markdown="1">

### Amazon, Google and Microsoft are making more from the AI labs than any VC

The VCs behind Anthropic will make billions from its IPO. But the cloud companies are set to make far more, and they get paid in three different ways.

- **As shareholders.** Amazon has put about $18 billion into Anthropic. Its own filings valued its Anthropic notes at $97.9 billion at the end of June, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3182406). [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com), one of its VC backers, holds a stake worth roughly $14 billion, by Bloomberg's figures. None of the VCs TechCrunch named owns 5% or more.
- **As suppliers.** Anthropic has committed to spend at least $110 billion on Amazon's cloud over ten years, TechCrunch reported. It has committed at least $114 billion more to Google's cloud.
- **As the shop.** Google's and Amazon's clouds resold about $7.8 billion of Anthropic's models in the first half. That's almost half its revenue, [per Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/info-1sghj5n-anthropics-s-1-takeaways-from-giant-compute-commitments-to-complex-lock/). Anthropic paid cloud resellers fees worth about 17% of its revenue.

OpenAI has the same arrangement with Microsoft. Microsoft recorded $10 billion in revenue from OpenAI in the quarter to September, about 11% of its total, [NewsBytes reported](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/microsoft-earned-10-billion-from-openai-in-q1-fiscal-2027/tldr). OpenAI reportedly shares 20% of its revenue with Microsoft.

**A VC makes money if the lab's shares go up. The cloud makes money every time the lab sells or spends.** That's why the clouds stand to gain more from the labs than the funds that picked them.

</div>

<div class="pattern" markdown="1">

### Upwind turned down Datadog because private investors offered as much, and Bedrock more than doubled its price in eight months

In our [first edition](/keeping-up-with-the-capital/editions/2026-10-09-anthropic-ipo-family-offices/), Listen Labs walked away from a $1.5 billion funding offer to sell itself instead. This week, a startup did the opposite.

[Upwind](https://www.upwind.io) sells software that watches companies' cloud systems while they run and stops attacks as they happen. Datadog tried to buy it. But Datadog wouldn't pay more than the $3.8 billion private investors were offering, so Upwind took the money instead, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/upwind-raises-400m-after-walking-away-from-datadog-acquisition-offer/). It raised $400 million, led by [Bessemer Venture Partners](https://www.bvp.com). Nine months ago, it was valued at $1.5 billion. Upwind expects to end the year at about $90 million in yearly recurring revenue, so investors paid more than 40 times that.

Private money is stretching just as far outside software. Bedrock Robotics fits its self-driving system onto ordinary excavators. It was founded in 2024 by former Waymo engineers. It raised [$600 million at a $4.1 billion valuation](https://thenextweb.com/news/bedrock-robotics-600m-series-c-4-1bn-valuation), co-led by [CapitalG](https://capitalg.com) and Emergence Capital. In February, it was valued at $1.75 billion. Since mid-August, its excavators have done paid work on live job sites with nobody in the cab.

**A buyout offer used to set a startup's ceiling. Now private investors can match it, and the startup stays independent.**

## India, Inc.

### India's listed startups are reporting profits: Ather, Paytm, Pine Labs and Shadowfax all made money last quarter
{: .take}

[Last week](/keeping-up-with-the-capital/editions/2026-10-23-anthropic-ipo-openai-bridge-round/), India's IPO buyers paid a big premium for profitable Tonbo Imaging, and Urban Company's shares fell when its loss tripled. This week, many of India's listed startups reported their September-quarter results. Most of them moved into profit, or closer to it.

[Ather Energy](https://www.atherenergy.com) swung to a ₹1.3 crore profit, against a ₹154 crore loss a year earlier, [per Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/ather-turns-profitable-in-q2-posts-%E2%82%B91-3-cr-pat/). Its shares rose almost 14% that day. But the profit is thin. Ather's sales were ₹61.5 crore short of its costs, and other income covered the gap.

The others were sturdier. [Paytm](https://paytm.com)'s profit rose more than ten times from a year ago, to ₹225 crore, [Inc42 reported](https://inc42.com/buzz/ather-turns-profitable-paytm-q2-pat-rockets-more/). [Pine Labs](https://www.pinelabs.com)' profit rose more than six times, and delivery company Shadowfax's more than five times, to ₹68 crore, [per Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/pine-labs-hits-new-high-ace-blend-bags-9-9-mn-more/). Even [Meesho](https://www.meesho.com), still loss-making, [cut its loss by 68%](https://inc42.com/buzz/meesho-q2-loss-narrows-68-yoy-to-₹132-cr-revenue-jumps-34-yoy/) while its revenue grew a third.

**India's stock market asked for profit. Its listed startups are starting to deliver it.**

### Investors are marking down their 2022 bets: one Baillie Gifford fund's books value Dailyhunt's parent about 90% below its peak
{: .take}

VerSe Innovation runs the news app Dailyhunt and the short-video app Josh. In April 2022, it raised $805 million at a reported $5 billion valuation. This week, [Inc42 reported](https://inc42.com/buzz/baillie-gifford-funds-slash-value-of-holdings-in-dailyhunt-parent-verse-by-up-to-80/) that two [Baillie Gifford](https://www.bailliegifford.com) funds had cut the value of their VerSe holdings by roughly 80% over the past two years.

One of them, the Schiehallion Fund, valued its 1.3% stake at $7.47 million in August. That implies VerSe is worth about $576 million, the Economic Times calculated. VerSe disputes it. It says the markdown doesn't reflect its overall valuation, and that other investors still value it at about $5 billion. But it hasn't raised a round that sets a new price since 2022.

SoftBank is shrinking an older bet, too. It has sold 5.55 crore Ola Electric shares since January, bringing its stake down from 12.9% to 11.7%, [per Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/softbank-sells-1-2-stake-in-ola-electric-offloads-1-12-cr-rights-entitlements/). It also sold about a quarter of its rights to buy new shares in Ola's ₹1,000 crore rights issue. Ola's revenue fell 45% in the June quarter.

Put this next to the story above. The startups that reached profit are being rewarded by the market. The ones that didn't are being repriced by their own investors. **A 2022 valuation is a memory, not a price.**
