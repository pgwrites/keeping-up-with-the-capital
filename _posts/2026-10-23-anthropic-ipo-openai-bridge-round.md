Hello there, stranger! 👀

Here for a sip?

Your favourite tea from Sand Hill Road to Koramangala is poured, and this week, everyone's whispering about who gets to put a price on AI: the stock market, or a handful of very rich investors.

Two editions ago, I wrote that Anthropic was heading for an IPO while OpenAI chose to stay private. This week, that choice got a price tag. One lab is about to show its numbers to anyone who wants to read them. The other is showing them to a small group of investors and asking for $30 billion. In India, the stock market made its own choices, and they were sharper than I expected. <mark>So who sets the price of a company: the crowd, or the few with the biggest cheques?</mark>

Let's get to it.

## The Big Sip

# Anthropic is about to open its books to the stock market, while OpenAI is raising $30 billion privately to wait
{: .take}

The two biggest AI labs need the same thing: huge amounts of money for computing power. This week, they went to get it in opposite ways.

[Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/) is heading for the stock market. It's expected to publicly file its IPO prospectus as soon as Friday, [Yahoo Finance reported](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/anthropic-to-publicly-file-for-ipo-as-early-as-friday-sources-say-135343841.html). That would clear the way for a listing in November. Wall Street expects it could raise as much as $100 billion, at a valuation of about $2 trillion.

Some of what's inside has already leaked. Anthropic lost $80.7 billion in the first half of 2026, [Benzinga reported](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/ipos/26/10/62517881/anthropic-could-take-major-ipo-step-as-soon-as-friday-what-investors-need-to-know) from the draft prospectus. That sounds alarming. But about $72 billion of it came from accounting charges, as the value of its convertible notes and other fundraising deals changed.

[OpenAI](https://openai.com/) went the other way. It's closing in on a round of up to $30 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation, led by investors from the UAE with [Coatue](https://www.coatue.com), [CNBC reported](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/openai-nears-30-billion-funding-round-at-125-trillion-valuation-4967451). OpenAI calls it an <mark>"opportunistic"</mark> bridge: money to keep spending until it lists, which its finance chief Sarah Friar has told staff will happen in 2027. The price slipped during the week. On Monday, Reuters reported OpenAI was seeking [more than $1.4 trillion](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-seeks-30-billion-funding-104159714.html). By Thursday, it was $1.25 trillion. That's still almost 50% more than the $852 billion investors paid in March.

**Anthropic is letting the market set its price. OpenAI is letting a few big investors set it.**

The prospectus will be the first time outsiders can check a frontier lab's books line by line. Tomasz Tunguz, a venture investor at Theory Ventures, [argued this week](https://tomtunguz.com/what-if-they-are-all-clouds/) that Anthropic should be valued like a cloud company, not an AI lab. On that view, $1.5–2 trillion matches his estimate for Google Cloud as a standalone business. But his own numbers show what investors will be checking. By his count, Anthropic has committed about eight times its yearly revenue run rate to data centres. Amazon, Microsoft and Google have committed one to two times theirs.

The public road isn't smooth, either. Earlier this month, Nvidia-backed data-centre builder Firmus [pulled its Australian IPO](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/startupsmart/firmus-shelves-44-billion-ipo-plan-citing-market-volatility/), citing market volatility. The biggest AI fundraises are splitting into two lanes: one where everyone reads the numbers, and one where a few investors read them first. If Anthropic's books hold up in public, waiting until 2027 will start to look costly for OpenAI.

## Connecting the Dots

<div class="pattern" markdown="1">

### Blacksmith, Legora and Flapping Airplanes were repriced within months: two on fast-growing revenue, one on a research bet

This week, three AI startups raised money at far higher prices than their last rounds. None of those last rounds was more than nine months old.

- **Blacksmith: from $550 million to $1 billion in about two months.** [Blacksmith](https://blacksmith.sh) runs the automated builds and tests that code must pass before it ships. It raised [$66 million at a $1 billion valuation](https://dealroom.co/news/164699-blacksmith-hits-1b-valuation-with-66m-series-c-led-by-sequoia/), led by [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com). Its yearly revenue has grown 17 times since January, to tens of millions of dollars. [Peak XV](https://www.peakxv.com) led its last round, in August, and India's [Premji Invest](https://www.premjiinvest.com) joined this one, [per Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2026/10/22/startup-funding-news-today-october-22-2026-flapping-airplanes-legora-mach9-peak-energy-whop-more/).
- **Legora: from $5.55 billion to $8.8 billion in seven months.** Sweden's [Legora](https://legora.com) makes AI software that helps lawyers research, review documents and draft. It raised [$550 million at an $8.8 billion valuation](https://qz.com/legora-series-e-550-million-valuation-legal-ai-102126) and brings in more than $200 million a year. In March, it was valued at [$5.55 billion](https://pulse2.com/legora-550-million-series-d-at-5-55-billion-valuation-raised-for-collaborative-ai-legal-platform/amp/).
- **Flapping Airplanes: four times its January price, a year after it started.** This year-old AI research lab wants to train capable models on far less data. It raised [$1 billion at a $6 billion valuation](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/year-old-ai-lab-flapping-airplanes-raises-1b-at-a-6b-valuation/), co-led by [Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com) and [Lux Capital](https://luxcapital.com). In January, its seed round valued it at $1.5 billion. Sequoia partner David Cahn, one of its investors, calls it the <mark>"young person's AGI lab."</mark>

Blacksmith and Legora show the logic. When revenue multiplies in months, a price set in the spring is out of date by autumn. Flapping Airplanes shows the other bet: investors paying up for a research team and an idea, with no revenue disclosed.

**When revenue grows in months, valuations reset in months.** The Indian angle is worth noticing, too. Peak XV's August cheque in Blacksmith nearly doubled in value on paper in about two months. Indian funds are no longer just backing Indian startups. They're getting into US AI deals early enough to catch these jumps.

## India, Inc.

### India's IPO buyers paid a 66% premium for profitable Tonbo Imaging and held back on loss-making Zetwerk
{: .take}

Last edition, two Bengaluru companies opened their IPOs on the same day. This week, both listed, and the market treated them very differently.

[Tonbo Imaging](https://tonboimaging.com) makes defence imaging and sensor systems. Its ₹852 crore IPO drew bids for [136 times the shares on offer](https://inc42.com/buzz/tonbo-imaging-makes-strong-market-debut-lists-at-66-premium-to-ipo-price/). On Friday, its shares opened at ₹1,162, 66% above the IPO price. That's despite revenue falling 22.7% last year. The difference is that Tonbo makes money: it earned [₹50.8 crore in profit](https://startuptalky.com/daily-indian-funding-roundup-key-news-19-october-2026/) in FY26.

[Zetwerk](https://www.zetwerk.com), the contract manufacturer, has the opposite profile. Its losses are shrinking, but it's [still loss-making](https://startuptalky.com/daily-indian-funding-roundup-key-news-19-october-2026/). Its IPO drew bids for [about three times the shares on offer](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/zetwerk-manufacturing-business-shares-make-decent-debut-stock-lists-with-5-gains), and individual investors bid for only 73% of their portion. It listed on Friday about 5% above its IPO price, worth about ₹25,100 crore. That's still below the ₹27,000 crore private investors paid in March. By mid-morning, the shares had [slipped below the IPO price](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/zetwerk-manufacturing-businesses-ipo-listing).

Listed startups got the same message. [Urban Company](https://www.urbancompany.com)'s loss more than tripled to ₹182.5 crore in the September quarter, as it spent heavily on InstaHelp, its quick household-help service. Its shares [fell almost 10% in a day](https://inc42.com/buzz/urban-company-slumps-9-5-after-q2-loss-widens-board-clears-₹2500-cr-fundraise/), and its board approved raising up to ₹2,500 crore.

The appetite is there. [Jio Platforms](https://www.jio.com)' ₹30,213 crore IPO, India's largest ever, was bid [more than 12 times over](https://hdfcsky.com/news/jio-ipo-subscribed-12-55x-by-3-pm-ist-on-day-3) by 3 PM on its last day. But Indian public investors are choosing carefully, and they're putting profit ahead of growth.

**Growth gets an Indian startup listed. Profit gets it a premium.**

### Indian startups are getting more than a third fewer cheques this year, but each one is much bigger
{: .take}

Indian startups raised [$10.9 billion from VCs](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/companies/news/companies-start-ups-india-vc-funding-stagnates-at-10-billion-startups-for-three-years-126101800347_1.html) between January and mid-October, Tracxn data shows. That's about the same as in each of the last two years. But the money went into 1,253 rounds, down from 1,982 a year earlier. So the average cheque grew from $5.1 million to $8.6 million, about 70% bigger.

This week's deals fit the pattern. Bengaluru's Even Healthcare runs its own hospitals and sells memberships that cover checkups, tests, insurance access and hospital stays. It raised a [$50 million Series B](https://inc42.com/buzz/even-healthcare-raises-50-mn-to-expand-hospital-network-across-bengaluru/), led by an existing investor, [Khosla Ventures](https://www.khoslaventures.com). Even says its valuation has tripled in 18 months. And Premji Invest, Norwest and Lok Capital have committed [up to ₹1,000 crore](https://lapaasvoice.com/premji-invest-norwest-and-lok-capital-invest-%E2%82%B91000-crore-in-vegafin-to-acquire-majority-stake-in-bizloan/) to Vegafin, a new small-business lender founded by former ICICI Bank executive Bijith Bhaskar. Vegafin has agreed to buy a majority stake in an existing lender, Bizloan.

Why fewer cheques? Anjan Ray, investment partner at deeptech investor Navam Capital, points to where VCs get their own money. He told [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/companies/news/companies-start-ups-india-vc-funding-stagnates-at-10-billion-startups-for-three-years-126101800347_1.html) that the people who fund VCs, such as family offices, financial institutions and wealthy individuals, are staying on the sidelines while the stock market is subdued. So VCs have less to spend, and they're spending it carefully.

**Indian VCs aren't spending less. They're spreading it across fewer companies.** The top 10 startups alone took about 36% of this year's money. For founders, being one of the few that investors already trust matters more than ever.
