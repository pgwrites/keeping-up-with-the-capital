Your favourite tea from Sand Hill Road to Koramangala is poured, and this week, everyone's whispering about how Anthropic's first morning on Nasdaq went from a 34% jump to a 13% slide in half an hour.

For seven weeks, this newsletter has followed one question: what price would the public put on a frontier AI lab? On Thursday, we got the answer. Then it kept changing, every second. Meanwhile, investors were writing AI-lab-sized cheques to health companies started by tech insiders, and India was asking, quite loudly, why it doesn't have an Anthropic of its own. <mark>Once a company has a price every second, who is it really priced for?</mark>

Let's get to it.

## The Big Sip

# Anthropic raised more money than any company ever has in an IPO, then gave back half its opening jump within 30 minutes
{: .take}

Last week, [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/) set a price range of $70 to $78 a share. By Sunday, orders were close to seven times the shares on offer, [The Next Web reported](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-ipo-prices-83-share-1-5-trillion-record-raise). So on Wednesday, it priced at $83, [above the range](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/tech/anthropic-ipo-83-share-ai-bubble/).

It also sold more shares than planned. Anthropic raised about $81.3 billion from the public, the biggest IPO ever, [Reuters reported](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/sci-tech/2026-11-19-anthropic-raises-record-81bn-in-ipo-at-15-trillion-valuation/). That tops SpaceX's record from June, [per The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-ipo-prices-83-share-1-5-trillion-record-raise). Add the $26 billion its partners bought privately, and the deal comes to $107.3 billion. On a fully diluted basis, Anthropic is valued at about $1.5 trillion.

Then trading started on Thursday. The first trade was at $111.50, 34% above the IPO price, [per INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/us-stocks/anthropic-stock-first-day-trading). Within 30 minutes, the stock had slid to about $97, a 13% drop that wiped out nearly $200 billion in market value, [Yahoo Finance reported](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/live/anthropic-ipo-live-anthropic-stock-begins-trading-in-record-81b-raise-with-15t-valuation-192410598.html). It called that the worst start for a big IPO since 2008. The stock closed at $97.17, still up 17% on the day. After hours, it fell about 5% more.

Small investors traded it heavily, buying and selling. <mark>"Retail are trading the stock hard... but taking both sides rather than a one-way bid,"</mark> said Viraj Patel, an analyst at Vanda Research.

Anthropic's venture backers, including Spark Capital, [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com), [Coatue](https://www.coatue.com) and [Lightspeed](https://lsvp.com), stand to gain, [Yahoo Finance noted](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/article/anthropic-ipo-what-we-know-143321078.html). But they can't cash in yet. Anthropic has 23 separate dates when lock-ups expire, [per Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/live/anthropic-ipo-live-anthropic-stock-begins-trading-in-record-81b-raise-with-15t-valuation-192410598.html). [Baillie Gifford](https://www.bailliegifford.com) put in about $1.5 billion before the IPO. <mark>"We bet that Anthropic has the ingredients,"</mark> said Peter Singlehurst, its head of private companies.

The harder test comes later. <mark>"Once Anthropic reports publicly, it becomes one of the clearest tests of whether the economics of AI are holding up,"</mark> said Armina Rosenberg, portfolio manager and co-founder of Minotaur Capital.

**Investors who got shares at $83 made 17% in a day. Investors who bought the first trade lost 13% in half an hour.**

## Connecting the Dots

<div class="pattern" markdown="1">

### Investors put $5.4 billion into two health companies born out of DeepMind and Neuralink this week, and only one has a product patients can use

On Tuesday, [Isomorphic Labs](https://www.isomorphiclabs.com) raised a $5 billion Series C, [Fierce Biotech reported](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/isomorphic-labs-raises-5b-series-c-months-after-21b-round). Greenoaks and [Sequoia Capital](https://www.sequoiacap.com) co-led, and Alphabet made what the company called a significant investment. Isomorphic was spun out of Google DeepMind in 2021 and is led by Demis Hassabis. It uses AI to design drug molecules, and has partnerships with Eli Lilly, Novartis and Johnson & Johnson.

The round is more than twice the $2.1 billion it raised in May. Its paper value is estimated at about $50 billion, [pharmaphorum reported](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/isomorphic-labs-raises-5bn-massive-third-round). But none of its drug candidates has reached a clinical trial yet. <mark>"Our drug design engine is generating molecules with unprecedented precision and speed,"</mark> Hassabis said.

The same day, Science Corporation raised a $400 million Series D at a reported $3.5 billion valuation, [The Next Web reported](https://thenextweb.com/news/science-corporation-400m-series-d-brain-computer-interface-prima). It was founded by Max Hodak, who co-founded Neuralink. Its PRIMA chip implant lets some people with age-related macular degeneration read letters and words again. PRIMA already has European approval and is available in Germany. [Khosla Ventures](https://www.khoslaventures.com) and Y Combinator are among the investors. The money will help Science seek approval in the US.

The two prices tell different stories. Isomorphic is valued on what its AI might discover. Science is valued on a device that patients already use.

**Tech money is moving into medicine, and the biggest cheque went to the company furthest from selling anything.**

### Two European startups that sell AI agents to finance teams raised $530 million this week, and the same fintech investor backed both

[Model ML](https://www.modelml.com) is a London startup that builds AI agents for bankers, consultants and investors. The agents run on each firm's own systems and data, and do their work inside Excel and PowerPoint. On Tuesday, it raised a $250 million Series B at a $1.5 billion valuation, co-led by [General Atlantic](https://www.generalatlantic.com) and QED Investors, [The Next Web reported](https://thenextweb.com/news/model-ml-250m-series-b-1-5bn-valuation-ai-agents-bankers). Its annual recurring revenue grew 12 times in a year.

A day later, [Payhawk](https://payhawk.com) raised $280 million at a $2 billion valuation, led by Goldman Sachs Alternatives, [The Next Web reported](https://thenextweb.com/news/payhawk-raises-280m-at-a-2bn-valuation-in-round-led-by-goldman-sachs). Payhawk was founded in Sofia in 2018 and is based in London. It sells company cards and software for expenses, invoices and buying, and now AI agents that handle purchases and payments. Its valuation has doubled since 2022. It turned cash-flow positive in the September quarter, and 77% of its customers use its AI agents every day. QED was in this round, too.

Why are finance teams paying first? Model ML's co-founder Arnie Englander told Bloomberg, per The Next Web: <mark>"These tools are no longer productivity and efficiency tools. They're revenue generative."</mark>

There's still a big gap between Europe and the US. Ramp, Payhawk's US rival, raised $1.85 billion at a $60 billion valuation last month, per The Next Web.

**Investors are paying for AI agents that do finance work because finance teams can see what that work earns.**

## India, Inc.

### Anthropic's listing started an Indian debate about building a rival, and this week's Indian model-builder raised $4 million
{: .take}

Uday Kotak, founder of Kotak Mahindra Bank, did the comparison on X, [Inc42 reported](https://inc42.com/buzz/anthropic-ipo-rekindles-debate-over-indias-ai-ambitions/). <mark>"This is more than the combined market cap of the top 10 Indian companies,"</mark> he wrote of Anthropic's valuation. <mark>"Are we undervalued, or do we need to catch up in the new world? Or both?"</mark>

Not everyone agreed it was a fair question. <mark>"Every disruption creates new companies with huge valuations. It is the nature of markets. Comparing is silly,"</mark> wrote Mohandas Pai, former CFO of Infosys.

The money gap is real, though. Indian AI startups raised a record $438 million across 35 deals in the September quarter, [per Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/anthropic-ipo-rekindles-debate-over-indias-ai-ambitions/). Anthropic raised more than 180 times that from the public in one day. And much of India's AI money goes to vertical AI, meaning products for one industry, rather than to companies building their own large models, [Inc42 noted](https://inc42.com/buzz/indias-anthropic-question-sharechats-turnaround-more/).

One of the few model-builders raised money this week. [Soket AI](https://soket.ai), founded in 2019, builds open-source AI models for Indian languages and is backed by the government's IndiaAI Mission. It recently launched EKA-1, a 26-billion-parameter model. This week, it raised $4 million from Bajaj Finance and Pennant Technologies, [Inc42 reported](https://inc42.com/buzz/soket-ai-raises-4-mn-from-bajaj-finserv-to-expand-its-ai-model-horizon/).

**India's model-builders are being funded by the government and corporates, not by VCs chasing the next Anthropic.**

### Two Indian AI startups raised $59 million this week by selling to Americans: one sells robot training data, the other a health assistant
{: .take}

[Humyn Labs](https://humynlabs.ai) was founded in Bengaluru in February. It builds its own two-camera rigs to record people doing everyday tasks, then labels the footage so robots and AI models can learn from it. It records in more than 20 countries, with Brazil, India and South Korea as the main hubs.

On Wednesday, it raised $47 million, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/accel-backs-indias-humyn-labs-to-supply-training-data-needed-by-u-s-robotics-ai-developers/). [Accel](https://www.accel.com) led $26 million of equity, with Prosus Ventures and Bajaj Finance's Finserv Intelligence. The other $21 million is venture debt. Humyn made $10 million in revenue between April and October, and about 90% of it came from the US. Its 12 paying customers include frontier AI labs and robotics companies. Co-founder Manish Agarwal used to run Nazara Technologies.

The same week, August AI raised about $12 million from Accel, [Peak XV](https://www.peakxv.com) and Claypond Capital for its health assistant built for US users, [Inc42 reported](https://inc42.com/buzz/indias-anthropic-question-sharechats-turnaround-more/). Its AI agent now phones doctors' offices to find and book appointments for patients, founder Anuruddh Mishra posted on X, [per FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-august-raises-12m-for-ai-agent-that-spent-405-hours-on-hold/).

There's a catch with selling data. Inc42 found in July that much of India's robot-data work is a <mark>"services contract with high volume, thin margins and no moat,"</mark> and that collection rates fell 30% in the first half of 2026, [per one industry executive](https://inc42.com/features/india-trains-the-worlds-robots-but-at-what-cost/).

**Humyn sells the raw material and August sells the finished product, but both get paid in dollars.**
