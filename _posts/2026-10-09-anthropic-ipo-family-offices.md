It's fun because, for the first time in years, the market is about to answer the question everyone's been arguing about over dinner: *what is frontier AI actually worth?*

## The Big Sip

# Anthropic will put a public price on frontier AI before OpenAI does
{: .take}

Picture two climbers on the same mountain. One stops, sets up base camp, and lets everyone see exactly how much oxygen it has burned. The other keeps climbing, funded by a small group of backers who agree on a price among themselves.

That's Anthropic and OpenAI right now.

[Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/)'s IPO prospectus, seen by Reuters, is the first real look inside a frontier lab. Revenue grew 12x in 2025 to [nearly $4.6 billion](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/anthropics-ipo-prospectus-shows-sweeping-ai-vision-surging-costs-2026-09-28/). The operating loss was $8.06 billion. For every $1 that came in, about $2.75 went out. Anthropic has also committed $518 billion to cloud and compute over the coming decade, more than 100x last year's revenue. Bloomberg says the roadshow could start [as soon as the week of 9 November](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/anthropic-said-to-target-mega-ipo-before-thanksgiving-holiday).

A lot of people will see those numbers and flinch. I'd read them the way you read any company building infrastructure before demand fully shows up. The spending always comes first. The real question is whether the revenue curve catches up.

[OpenAI](https://openai.com/) took the other path. Sam Altman says there will be [no IPO in 2026](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/openai-seeks-30b-at-1-4t-valuation-as-ipo-waits). Instead, OpenAI is reportedly raising [at least $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-29/openai-targets-30-billion-in-new-funding-at-1-4-trillion-value), 64% above its March price. Dealroom puts a $30 billion round in the top 1% of more than 1,250 late-stage US enterprise software rounds.

Here's the part I find fascinating. You'd expect investors to prefer the lab that stays private and keeps marking itself up. They don't. In [Newcomer's September survey](https://reports.newcomer.co/) of 25 VCs, 76% said they'd buy Anthropic shares. Only 40% said the same of OpenAI. In the same survey, 17% think an AI bubble probably bursts soon.

So far, a handful of private investors have set the price of frontier AI. In November, the public market gets a vote. [Newcomer](https://www.newcomer.co/p/anthropics-ipo-cant-wait-forever) called the coming weeks <mark>"very instructive in assessing the health of the AI business."</mark> I'd go a step further. Whatever public investors pay for Anthropic becomes the yardstick for OpenAI's $1.4 trillion, whether OpenAI likes it or not.

## Connecting the Dots

<div class="pattern" markdown="1">

### This week's biggest AI valuation jumps are offers, not closed rounds

A price and a bid are different things. A bid is someone saying "I'd pay this." A price is someone actually paying it. This week brought a lot of very loud bids:

- [Etched](https://www.etched.com/) designs AI chips. It was valued at $10.3 billion in July and $21 billion a few weeks later. Now TechCrunch reports it's fielding [offers at $40–50 billion](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/etched-fields-funding-offers-at-40b-valuation-sources-say/), nearly 5x its July price. It does have something to point to: $1 billion in customer orders, announced in July.
- [Kalshi](https://kalshi.com/) runs a regulated prediction market. It was valued at $22 billion in May and is now [in talks at around $40 billion](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/tiger-global-dragoneer-talks-invest-in-kalshis-new-1-billion-fundraise-sources-2026-09-29/). It handled [$60.7 billion in trades in September](https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/volume/kalshi/), 78% of the market. The asterisk: CNBC [found clusters of near-identical trades](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/kalshi-polymarket-trading-volume-scrutiny.html) in some of its products.
- [FieldAI](https://www.fieldai.com/) builds the brains that run robots. It went from $2 billion last year to a signed term sheet [at $10 billion](https://www.businessinsider.com/robotics-startup-fieldai-raises-at-10b-valuation-in-new-round-2026-10), with more than $135 million in revenue and signed contracts.

Zoom out and the money is concentrating. Crunchbase counted [a record 27 rounds of $1 billion or more in Q3](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/q3-2026-global-startup-funding-ai-billion-dollar-rounds-exits-data/), together taking about a third of the $159 billion raised worldwide. Indian capital is playing too: Premji Invest co-led a [$145 million Series C](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/biggest-funding-rounds-ai-cyber-real-estate-instinct/) in CScale, a US company that makes optical links for AI servers.

But a price only counts once someone can sell at it. PitchBook counted [just 18 US IPOs in Q3](https://pitchbook.com/news/reports/q3-2026-pitchbook-nvca-venture-monitor). Most of these numbers have only been agreed by the people already in the room. Anthropic is the first one walking out the door to find out what the rest of the world thinks.

</div>

<div class="pattern" markdown="1">

### A $30 million-revenue AI startup sold for more than Airtable

If one set of deals explains software in 2026, it's this one.

- [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/) [agreed to buy](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/salesforce-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-listen-labs/) [Listen Labs](https://listenlabs.com/), which uses AI agents to run customer research, for a reported $2 billion. That's about 67x its roughly $30 million in annual revenue. Listen Labs [walked away from a signed term sheet](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/ai-research-startup-listen-labs-scrubbed-a-1-5b-funding-round-for-salesforce-talks/) from Menlo Ventures at $1.5 billion to take the deal.
- Bending Spoons, the company that buys established apps like Evernote and Vimeo, picked up [Airtable](https://www.airtable.com/) for [$1.28 billion in cash](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/04/bending-spoons-to-buy-airtable-for-1-28b/), about [2.7x revenue](https://dealroom.co/resources/airtable-bending-spoons/). Airtable earns more than 15x what Listen Labs does, and it was valued above $11 billion in 2021.
- Five weeks later, Bending Spoons agreed to buy [Miro](https://miro.com/) for $1.355 billion, [90% below its $17.5 billion peak](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2026/09/10/bending-spoons-buys-miro-at-90-discount-as-saas-unicorns-sell-for-scraps/).
- [EliseAI](https://www.eliseai.com/), which automates the admin of rental housing, raised at [$4 billion](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/29/3370681/0/en/eliseai-raises-350-million-at-4-billion-valuation-to-bring-ai-deeper-into-housing-and-healthcare-operations.html), about 20x its $200 million-plus in ARR. Value Add VC's Trace Cohen says it earned that [<mark>"on usage, not narrative."</mark>](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/eliseai-350m-round-4b-valuation-2026)

That's a 25-fold gap in revenue multiples between Listen Labs and Airtable, and Airtable is the far bigger company. So size can't explain it.

Here's how I think about it: **Salesforce bought a slope. Bending Spoons bought a cash flow.** A young AI product is priced on where it could go. A 2021-era SaaS company is priced like a mature business that throws off cash, which is exactly what Bending Spoons likes to buy.

For software founders, that's the whole game now. Revenue sets the floor. The growth story sets the price.

</div>

<div class="pattern" markdown="1">

### As AI models get cheaper, the fight moves to who owns the customer

Think of AI as a three-layer cake: the models at the bottom, the apps in the middle, and the customer on top. The big question for the next five years is which layer gets to eat.

Pranav Pai, founding partner at Bengaluru's 3one4 Capital (about $570 million in committed capital), thinks the labs are coming for the top layer: [<mark>"The most profitable move in artificial intelligence may no longer be selling intelligence."</mark>](https://inc42.com/resources/selling-intelligence-was-the-warm-up-for-ai-owning-vertical-markets-may-be-the-play/) His evidence: Anthropic launched Claude Design after partnering with Figma, and Claude for Legal after featuring Harvey as a customer. OpenAI now sells Presence, its own support agent, after Intercom built its agents on OpenAI's models.

State Street Global Advisors sees it the other way. In a [September note](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/insights/beyond-deepseek-chinas-2026-model-wave-and-the-repricing-of-the-ai-stack), it argues the lead among model makers changes hands so often that their moats are thinning, and that <mark>"enterprises and software deployers, by contrast, should keep a rising share of the surplus."</mark> Theory Ventures' Tomasz Tunguz shows the pressure underneath both views: model prices have [fallen 41% while usage rose 50%](https://tomtunguz.com/what-if-the-models-are-commoditized/).

Both camps agree on one thing: the model alone is becoming a weak moat. If Pai is right, the safe ground is deep industry knowledge the labs can't see from outside. If State Street is right, it's owning the customer relationship. Either way, the worst seat in the house is a thin wrapper on someone else's model.

India has more riding on this than most. Pai points out that Indian software firms and the country's 1,900-plus global capability centres are among the heaviest users of these models. In his view, that shows the labs exactly how their clients work.

</div>

## India, Inc.

### India's largest startup round of Q3 had no VC in it
{: .take}

Here's a stat I keep coming back to. India's biggest startup round of the quarter didn't include a single venture fund.

Electric-scooter maker [Simple Energy](https://www.simpleenergy.in/) raised [about $180 million in a Series C](https://inc42.com/buzz/from-simple-energy-to-arivihan-indian-startups-raised-over-233-mn-this-week/). It was led by the family office of A. Velumani, founder of the diagnostics chain Thyrocare, alongside another family office and a few angels. That one round is about 6% of everything Indian startups raised in Q3, which came to [just under $3 billion](https://entrackr.com/report/weekly-funding-report-weekly-funding-report/funding-and-acquisitions-in-indian-startups-this-week-sep-28-oct-3-12618694), down from $3.5 billion in Q2 and $3.87 billion in Q1.

Why would family money step in where VCs stepped back? Time. A VC fund has a clock: it must return its investors' money within a fixed fund life. A family office has no such clock. Sharrp Ventures' Rishabh Mariwala put it beautifully: <mark>"With a family office, it is a marriage without a divorce date."</mark> For something as capital-hungry as building EVs, that patience is worth a lot.

And this isn't a one-off. Indian family offices have joined more than 1,300 startup deals since 2014, and their number grew from 45 in 2018 to about 300 in 2024, [per Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/magazine/india-built-a-start-up-ecosystem-on-foreign-capital-family-offices-may-now-have-to-keep-it-rolling). As VC money shrinks, Indian founders building capital-heavy companies now have a second, domestic source of growth capital. That's a big deal.

### Indian chip startups are being funded by VCs, corporates and the government together
{: .take}

[Agrani Labs](https://www.agranilabs.com/) builds AI computing hardware and software for data centres. Four former Intel and AMD engineers founded it in Bengaluru in 2024. It came out of stealth in February with an [$8 million seed](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/indian-chip-startup-agrani-labs-emerges-from-stealth-with-8m-funding-round/) from Peak XV. Eight months later, it's reportedly raising [₹800–850 crore from Samsung, 360 One and Peak XV](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/agrani-in-talks-to-raise-rs-800-850-crore-from-samsung-360-one-peak-xv/articleshow/134622670.cms), more than 10x its seed. It's also applying for another ₹120–130 crore from the government's research and innovation (RDI) scheme.

Chips are a brutal business to fund, because you need years of capital before you ship anything. Agrani's answer is to split that weight three ways: a VC, a strategic corporate and the state. It isn't alone. Accel, Lightspeed, Bessemer and others have joined [DeepVerse](https://indianstartupnews.com/news/accel-meta-intel-and-ather-join-30-member-deepverse-platform-to-boost-indias-deeptech-ecosystem-12629528), a 30-member group set up to shape the rules for slow-to-build sectors and feed into the same ₹1 lakh crore RDI scheme. If Agrani closes with the government money attached, Indian deeptech founders get a template to copy.

### Indian IPO investors are paying for scale, not brand: Moneyview soared, Snapdeal's parent sank
{: .take}

Two IPOs, days apart, with opposite outcomes.

Digital lender [Moneyview](https://moneyview.in/)'s ₹1,092 crore IPO was subscribed about 100x, and the stock [opened more than 60% above](https://entrackr.com/report/weekly-funding-report-weekly-funding-report/funding-and-acquisitions-in-indian-startups-this-week-sep-28-oct-3-12618694) its ₹34 issue price.

Snapdeal's parent AceVector drew 4.93x demand for its ₹420 crore IPO, then [listed 11.6% below](https://inc42.com/buzz/snapdeal-parent-acevector-lists-at-11-6-discount-on-bse/) its ₹32 issue price on BSE and closed its first day 18.4% down. This was once a $6.5 billion company, and its FY26 loss narrowed 64% to ₹45.5 crore. Investors still passed. Inc42's [analysis](https://inc42.com/features/snapdeal-makes-it-to-d-street-but-wheres-the-moat/) shows why in one comparison: Snapdeal had 12.16 million annual transacting users in FY26. Meesho had 264 million.

The lesson for India's IPO pipeline: a famous name and a smaller loss won't carry you. Scale will. Three more companies are lining up to test that:

- **StockGro**, an investing-education app, has filed confidentially for an IPO of [up to ₹2,500 crore](https://inc42.com/buzz/from-simple-energy-to-arivihan-indian-startups-raised-over-233-mn-this-week/), ₹800 crore of it in new shares.
- **Tonbo Imaging**, a defence-tech company, has SEBI's approval for a listing in which existing shareholders [sell up to 1.81 crore shares](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moodys:613c6053f815e:0-defence-electronics-maker-tonbo-imaging-receives-sebi-approval-for-ipo/). The company itself raises nothing.
- **Kuku**, an audio-series app, [turned profitable in FY26](https://inc42.com/buzz/ipo-bound-kuku-technologies-turns-profitable-in-fy26-revenue-surges-6x-to-%e2%82%b91484-cr/) on ₹1,484 crore of revenue and is getting ready to list.
